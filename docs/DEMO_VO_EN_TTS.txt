This A I agent was told to pay an attacker. The contract said no. No funds moved, and the agent's nonce was not consumed.

TabWrit gives merchants and A I agents a capped, expiring, revocable spending Tab. A prompt injection hits the contract, not your wallet.

The owner funds a vault once. Every owner action is signed in their own wallet. The server never holds the owner key.

For each merchant or agent, the owner opens a Tab: a per-charge cap, a period budget, a lifetime cap, an expiry, and a receiver policy. The spender gets a function call key scoped to charge. It cannot withdraw, pause, revoke, or change the rule.

The customer does not sign again. The merchant's scoped key signs a N E P three sixty-six delegate off-chain, and the relayer pays the gas. The merchant's Near balance stays unchanged. The contract still enforces the caps and consumes the nonce.

These are not simulated results. Twenty-three adversarial checks are real testnet transactions. The final vault has zero access keys. There is no deployer backdoor.

An unpaid A P I request returns a real H T T P four oh two invoice. The M C P tool pays it from the agent's Tab. The A P I returns data only after verifying the vault's charge executed event. Replay, wrong payer, over-cap, a redirected receiver, and a revoked Tab are all rejected. Thirteen out of thirteen.
